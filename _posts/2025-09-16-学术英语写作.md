An experimental research report is a paper written by an investigator to describe a completed research study.
Its purpose is to explain to others in the same field the objectives, methods, and findings of the study. It may be published in a professional journal, appear as a monograph, or be written as a thesis or dissertation to fulfill degree requirements.

They are designed around a **research question**. A **hypothesis** is formulated as a possible answer to the research question. They are typically **quantitative**, dealing with numerical data. Data are analyzed using **statistical tests** to evaluate the significance of the results.

Abstract, introduction, method, results, discussion.

## 5 Stages of introduction (🔥必考🔥)
1. First Stage: General statements about a field of research to provide the reader with a ***setting/background***(背景) for the problem to be reported.
2. Second Stage (***Literature review***): More specific statements about the aspects of the problem already studied by other researchers.
3. Third Stage (***Gap, 前人研究的空白***): Statements that indicate the need for more investigation.
4. Fourth Stage: Very specific statements giving the ***purpose/objective*** of the writer's study.
5. Fifth Stage: Optional statements that give a ***value*** or justification for carrying out the study.

## How to choose appropriate verb voice (动词的语态，即主动或被动语态) in describing the procedures of method section?
The choice between active and passive voice depends on:

1. **Depersonalization**: The passive voice is conventionally used to emphasize the procedure rather than the researcher, allowing omission of the agent (e.g., "I" or "we").

2. **Information Structure**: Place old information at the beginning of the sentence and new information at the end. Use active or passive voice to achieve this flow.

3. **Style Preferences**: Some editors or professors may prefer active voice for a more personal style.


## What are the three kinds of sentences commonly used in procedural descriptions? How to shorten them?
1. **Compound sentences with the same subject and passive verbs**  
   - Full: The data were collected and they were analyzed.  
   - Short: The data were collected and analyzed.  
   - **Shorten by omitting the subject and *be* auxiliary in the second part.**

2. **Compound sentences with different subjects and passive verbs**  
   - Full: The data were collected and correlations were calculated.  
   - Short: The data were collected and correlations calculated.  
   - **Shorten by omitting the *be* auxiliary before the second verb.**

3. **Sentences with *which* clauses containing passive verbs**  
   - Full: The data which were obtained were analyzed.  
   - Short: The data obtained were analyzed.  
   - **Shorten by dropping *which* and the *be* auxiliary.**

## What is the convention that most writers follow in writing discussion section?
The convention that most writers follow in writing the discussion section is the progressive move from specific to more general information elements.


## The kinds of information that you can include in your discussion section are not fixed. However, the first elements are typically those that refer most directly to the study and its findings. What are they?
The first information elements in the discussion section, which refer most directly to the study and its findings, are:
   - A reference to the main purpose or hypothesis of the study;
   - A review of the most important findings, whether or not they support the original hypothesis, and whether they agree with the findings of other researchers;
   - Possible explanations for or speculations about the findings;
   - Limitations of the study that restrict the extent to which the findings can be generalized.


## As the discussion section continues, the writer moves the reader's attention away from the specific results of the study and begins to focus more generally on the importance that the study may have for other workers in the field. What are the later information elements in discussion?
The later information elements in the discussion section, which focus more generally on the importance of the study for the field, are:
   - Implications of the study (generalizations from the results);
   - Recommendations for future research and practical applications.


## What tenses are used in different kinds of information in discussion section?
The tenses used in different kinds of information in the discussion section are:
   - For the first information elements (specific reference to the study): Simple Past Tense is most commonly used for referring to the purpose, hypothesis, and findings. Past, Present, or Modal Auxiliaries may be used for explaining findings and stating limitations.
   - When comparing findings with other researchers: Present Tense.
   - For later information elements (general statements about the study): Simple Present Tense and Modal Auxiliaries/Tentative Verbs are used for implications, recommendations, and applications.

## Abstracts from all fields of study are written ina very similar way. The types of information included and their order are very conventioanal(依照习惯的)? What are the typical information elements in an abstract? What order is followed? What does each letter of "BPMRC" stand for?
Abstracts from all fields of study generally follow a conventional structure with five typical information elements, often abbreviated as **BPMRC**. The standard order is:

- **B** = Background information  
- **P** = Principal activity (or purpose) of the study and its scope  
- **M** = Methodology used in the study  
- **R** = Most important results of the study  
- **C** = Conclusion or recommendation


## What are typical types of information in reduced abstract? 
In a reduced abstract, the information is condensed and typically includes:

- **P + M** = Purpose and method of the study (combined)  
- **R** = Results  
- **C** = Conclusions and recommendations (optional)

Background information (B) is usually eliminated in reduced abstracts.


## What tenses are usually used in writing different types of information in an abstract?
The typical verb tenses used for each element in an abstract are:

- **B (Background)**: Present tense  
- **P (Principal activity/Purpose)**: Past tense or present perfect tense  
- **M (Methodology)**: Past tense  
- **R (Results)**: Past tense  
- **C (Conclusion)**: Present tense, often with tentative verbs or modal auxiliaries
